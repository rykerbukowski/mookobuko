## Romania!

Romania was another country that had been on my bucket list for a long time; a lifelong obsession with vampires and the supernatural will do that to a girl. Naturally, this led to me wanting to do a deeper dive into the country’s history, which led me to *Children of the Night: The Strange and Epic Story of Modern Romania* by Paul Kenyon, which came out in 2021. I would highly recommend it if you’re interested in Romanian history. This book was the perfect launch pad; the author takes into account readability and the art of storytelling, instead of dull statistic after dull statistic.

We landed in Bucharest and rushed to greet Janine (Ryker’s mom), who had arrived the day before. It was a happy reunion with our favorite drug mule!

Our time in Bucharest kicked off with the most petty fight you can ever imagine with our Airbnb host, and it was all about the heat. Mind you, we were not booking a cheap place. We were in the middle of the “happening” part of Bucharest during their busiest season.

The temperature while we were in Bucharest was peaking around 90-95 degrees in the afternoon. Doesn’t sound too crazy to some of you Americans… well - I’ve GOT SOME NEWS FOR YOU. Even though we booked this Airbnb specifically because it had THREE AC units, well - one of them didn’t work, and the other two felt as if they were just acting as FANS, not as AC units. Which is a very difficult concept to explain to a snooty Romanian Airbnb host whose second language is English… they will say, “See, it is working” - as it is blowing warm air at you.

Well, we went back and forth with this host, telling her that it was not getting cool in the apartment. I’m lucky to have the most sensitive sleeper in the world as a wife, and I was not going to settle until this was made right! The host assured us that it was working perfectly, that 26-28 degrees C is a perfectly reasonable temperature to sleep in, and that it’s the temperature Romanians sleep in… So - for those who do not know this strange temperature scale, that is 79-82 degrees F for sleeping! ### Transylvania and Bran Castle

After Bucharest, Meg, Mom, and I hopped on a train and headed into the mountains of Transylvania to visit the famous Bran Castle.

<img loading="lazy" src="/media/posts/21/gallery/train-meg-janine.jpg" alt="Meg and Janine smiling in their train seats" />

What makes Bran Castle so exciting is how oddly inaccurate it actually is. See, Bran Castle is famous for being the inspiration for Dracula’s castle in Bram Stoker’s novel. The kicker here is that Bram was an Irishman who never actually went to Romania. In addition, the owners of the castle don’t live in Romania either - they are Austrians. Now, you can at least have the truest of inspirations for the castle through Vlad the Impaler, right? The mythic figure who was also the inspiration for Dracula, right?? Well, see, Vlad (who, by the way, is actually very interesting and was, yes, a cruel guy - but he got shit done; I will explain more shortly) is barely connected to this castle at all. The only connection is that the castle might have been a military post during Vlad’s time, and Vlad may or may not have stopped here for a night or two on his way through.

Now for Vlad. Here is his most interesting story: in 1462 the Ottoman sultan, Mehmed II, invaded Wallachia. This is the same Mehmed II who conquered Constantinople. So… a fairly serious situation. Vlad had a much smaller army, so instead of fighting head-on, he destroyed supplies the invaders could use, harassed them along the way, and made every mile of their trip miserable. As the Ottomans approached Vlad’s capital, they found thousands of bodies displayed on stakes. An entire “forest of the impaled.” You can imagine what that did to an army that was already exhausted and struggling for supplies. The Ottomans packed up their shit and turned right around. (It didn’t last very long, however. The sultan backed Vlad’s brother, and Vlad lost the throne later that same year.)

### Kraków

We left Budapest and headed to Poland! While in Kraków, we visited the Wieliczka Salt Mine, which began mining rock salt in the 13th century! Salt was used for preserving food before refrigeration, so it was extremely in demand and valuable. In the 14th century, the salt mine accounted for ⅓ of the king’s revenues!! It was extremely important. The mine extracted salt continuously for roughly 700 years. It is now primarily used as a tourist destination and a spa.

Some other fun facts about the mine: between the 1500s and 1600s, there were roughly 2,000 workers who lived and worked in the mine at any given time. It’s enormous, with 240 km of passages, 2,400 chambers, and a depth of 330 m. There are salt chandeliers, horses worked underground, and there’s even a church!
