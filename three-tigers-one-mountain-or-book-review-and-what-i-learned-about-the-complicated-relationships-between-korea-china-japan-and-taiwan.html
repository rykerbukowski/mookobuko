Three Tigers: One Mountain | Book Review and What I Learned about the Complicated Relationships Between Korea, China, Japan, and Taiwan

November 6, 2025

Korea hates Japan.  

Taiwan hates Korea. 

China dislikes Korea and Japan and claims Taiwan. 

and Japan is walking around with their hands up saying, "Can't we all just move on from WWII?"

I had no idea the state of the relationships between these three major Eastern powers before reading Michael Booth's Three Tigers, One Mountain: A Journey Through the Bitter History and Current Conflicts of China, Korea, and Japan. In general I would not consider myself well versed on history or modern day politics, but as my year-long world trip looms, I figured that I needed to start somewhere. 

Our trip started in Tokyo and so learning a bit about Japan's history was a good place to start, the problem? Starting to dive into history with a very shaky foundation proved difficult. All the books, articles, movies and more seemed to be torn straight out of academia. Some PDH looking to publish, "for the public" forgetting the public is very different from their collection of pretentious colleagues. And I needed the basics, but I'm a picky reader. I also wanted to be entertained, to absorb and understand what I was reading, not another list of names and dates followed but a way too long explanation of one event, I needed the big picture - enter Michael Booth. 

From what I gather Michael Booth is a food writer. Previously publishing a couple books on the history of Japan, none of which I'd read. But when I found Three Tigers I knew it had the potential to satiate my curiosity. Three Tigers follows Booth as he explores around Japan, Korea, China, and finally Taiwan. Each chapter takes place in a new city, following a similar flow: a modern day expose, historical context, and interviews, with all sorts of folks, from students to scholars.
